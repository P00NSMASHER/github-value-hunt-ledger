## Post 1 — Freight audit checklist

**Hook:** A freight invoice can add up correctly and still be wrong.

Before calling a charge valid, finance/AP teams should be able to answer:
- Which contract, tariff, rate confirmation, or agreed source controls the charge?
- Does the shipment evidence support the billed service?
- Are accessorial triggers actually documented?
- Was the correct rate version effective on the shipment date?
- Has the carrier already issued an automatic credit?
- Is the discrepancy genuinely new, or already known to an incumbent auditor?

## Post 2 — Accessorial evidence

**Hook:** Detention and accessorial charges are where operational facts and commercial rules collide.

A defensible audit should keep these separate:
1. What physically happened?
2. What commercial rule controlled the charge?
3. What amount should have been billed?
4. Was a credit already issued?
5. Was money actually recovered?

When those layers get collapsed, false-positive recovery claims appear.

## Post 3 — Potential recovery is not recovered money

**Hook:** A $25,000 billing discrepancy is not automatically a $25,000 recovery.

Freight Recovery separates:
- potential recovery;
- validated finding;
- approved claim;
- issued credit/refund;
- actual recovered funds;
- later reversal.

That distinction protects both the buyer and the audit process from inflated savings claims.

## Post 4 — Duplicate freight invoices

**Hook:** Duplicate freight billing is easy to describe and surprisingly easy to mishandle.

Exact duplicates are only one case. Near-duplicates can involve:
- same shipment, different invoice numbers;
- rebills after credits;
- partial reversals;
- duplicate accessorial lines;
- duplicate remittance application;
- carrier-corrected invoices that coexist with the original.

A clean audit should preserve the relationship between the original charge, correction, payment, credit, and final economic state.

## Post 5 — What to gather before an audit

If your finance or logistics team wants to review freight billing, start with:
- invoice export;
- rate agreements or rate cards;
- shipment/BOL/POD evidence;
- carrier statements;
- payment and credit history;
- prior audit output;
- a defined date range.

Do **not** send records to an unknown public form. Start with fit and scope, then use an approved secure intake route.
