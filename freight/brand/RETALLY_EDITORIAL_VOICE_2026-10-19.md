# RETALLY editorial voice and publication guard — 19 October 2026

**Purpose:** Make RETALLY's website, educational articles and organic media more useful to AP, finance and transportation buyers without introducing unsupported performance claims.

**Status:** Editorial house guide only. This file does not authorize any outreach, public post, publisher submission, site deployment, customer-data transfer or paid placement.

## Voice

- **Lead with a business decision:** a concrete invoice question, a practical source check or a tool the reader can use.
- **Write for a working controller:** direct verbs, recognizable artifacts (bill of lading, contract version, gate log, credit posting) and short paragraphs.
- **Be confident about the method, conditional about the result.** Explain what evidence could show; do not suggest RETALLY has validated customer savings that it has not.
- **Avoid confrontational slogans:** "fake savings," "fairy-tale recovery," and all-caps accusations should not be routine brand voice.
- **Offer one action per post:** save a checklist, read an example, compare measurements or request a non-sensitive fit conversation. Never request invoices or payment information in DMs.
- **Vary the story shape:** a single worked example; three source records; contract-change question; preventive handoff; free tool; or buyer FAQ. Do not retell the same four-stage recovery caution in every post.

## Financial definitions and accuracy

1. **Candidate difference** — an observation, not an overcharge or savings.
2. **Supported finding** — source-linked shipment facts and controlling commercial terms justify a reviewer conclusion.
3. **Authorized action** — a separate customer approval, not an assumed consequence of a finding.
4. **Realized recovery** — an eligible credit/refund/offset actually received, reconciled against reversals, exclusions and prior known findings.

Never report historic public cases, fictional invoices, original third-party study numbers, price estimates, posted carrier credits from other sources or planned software functions as RETALLY customer outcomes.

## Evidence and attribution

- For fictional examples, mark "illustrative" once where readers see the figure. Do not scatter repeated warnings.
- For outside case studies or statistics, attribute them to the author/source and state whether they are vendor-reported or independently verified.
- If presenting a product capability, verify that the specific page/tool exists and distinguish a comparison/calculator from binding rate, classification or refund determinations.
- Keep author photos authentic to the publisher's requirements; disclose AI-generated or materially altered images and never describe them as unaltered photographs.
- Keep the site canonical URL and each post's UTM tags unchanged during a copy revision unless analytics ownership approves the change.
- Confirm the exact service and price language against the current approved commercial terms. The agreed eligible initial audit is $0 upfront; any recovery action requires separate written authorization and qualifying realized value.

## Editorial preparation and release sequence

1. **Draft**: clearly label material as new, revised, already submitted or publisher-requested revision.
2. **Verify**: check source statements, editor requirements, image rights, links, examples, byline and publishing deadline.
3. **Owner approval**: obtain approval before an external email, publisher submission, new social publication, or paid placement.
4. **Live evidence**: distinguish sent, technically screened, accepted and published. Record a public permalink before reporting coverage.
5. **Measure**: qualified buyer conversation and securely handled inquiry are leading commercial outcomes. Clicks and impressions are supporting context only.

**Scheduled-post safeguard:** Content revisions to already scheduled posts change what will publish later. Prepare replacements separately and obtain explicit approval for applying them; preserve existing post UUID, date/time, media, tags and account after approval. A failed or disappeared scheduled item is not proof it was published.

## Writing examples

| Weak or repetitive | Buyer-oriented alternative |
| --- | --- |
| We don't charge for wishful thinking. | Recovery fees are tied to eligible amounts actually recovered under signed terms. |
| A line item isn't evidence. | Three records to check before disputing a liftgate charge. |
| Stop counting every flag as savings. | Where did the credit or refund actually post? |
| A freight invoice can total perfectly and still deserve a closer look. | The numbers add up. Does the contracted rate match? |

**Out-of-scope:** This guide does not authorize claims about commercial readiness, platform benchmarks, independent customer recoveries, industry leadership or publisher endorsements.
