# RETALLY — Facebook and Instagram Community Management Playbook
_Operational copy and escalation rules · October 11, 2026_

## Operating position
**Brand line:** Find it. Prove it. Recover it.
**What RETALLY does:** Reviews freight invoices against applicable rate terms, shipment/service evidence and prior adjustments; documents possible issues; assists with separately authorized recovery work.
**Offer boundaries:** Initial audit is $0 upfront for agreed eligible scope. A recovery engagement requires separate written terms and approval. Fees under the flagship contingency offer attach only to eligible actual recovered funds, not uncollected estimates. Results are never guaranteed.

**Audience:** Shipper and distributor decision-makers; AP managers, controllers, finance leaders, transportation managers, procurement and logistics operators.

## Voice and creative guardrails
- Write as a precise, useful freight-finance specialist. Short sentences, clear distinctions, one real insight per reply. Confident and modern; not salesy, meme-heavy or combative.
- Invite productive conversation without fishing for personal information or making claims of proven savings.
- Do not present examples, sample reports, prior credits or estimated findings as actual client results.
- Never claim years in business, client volume, savings, partnerships, legal credentials, industry certifications, guarantees, or a staffed 24-hour service without independent evidence.
- Original approved logo assets only. The original RETALLY PNG wordmark and emblem are the source of truth. Never use retired WebP logo derivatives.
- Never post a private invoice, carrier account number, contract, payment detail or customer identifier on social media. No freight files in DMs or ordinary email. Agree an approved secure route separately before receiving sensitive records.

## Daily launch handling (human-reviewed, no bots)
1. When a scheduled post goes live, confirm its actual public permalink, timestamp, image and text on the **real platform**. A Metricool PENDING card is not evidence of publication.
2. Check meaningful new comments, mentions and messages; prioritize actual buyer questions. Target a same-business-day response when feasible (not a promised SLA).
3. Reply once with useful information and one next action. Do not publish repetitive canned responses to every comment.
4. Move any sensitive specifics offline **without requesting files**. Only basic, non-sensitive scope details belong in the public first conversation.
5. Record real outcomes: public question handled, website action, verified inquiry sent/received, buyer qualification, signed authorization. Keep these distinct; no inferred leads from reach or clicks.
6. Escalate confidential-data mistakes, disputes, threats, suspected impersonation, legal/compliance questions, or complaints to the operator for careful human review. Do not automatically delete legitimate criticism.

## Approved response bank — adapt to each person's question

**“What do you actually do?”**
“We give freight invoices a second look against the governing rates, shipment records and prior credits. If something looks unsupported, we document the evidence before recommending a next step. The initial bounded audit is $0 upfront. Our approach is at retallyrecovery.com.”

**“How is the audit free?”**
“The initial review has no upfront fee for agreed eligible scope. If a supportable issue is found, you decide separately whether to authorize recovery work under written terms. Any recovery fee is based on eligible amounts actually recovered; results aren't guaranteed.”

**“We already have an auditor/TMS.”**
“That can be a good starting point. Our Second-Look Audit accounts for findings, credits and claims your existing process already identified. The goal is supportable **net-new** issues—not taking credit for somebody else's work: retallyrecovery.com/second-look-freight-audit.”

**“Can you look at our LTL/parcel invoices?”**
“Potentially. It depends on the carriers, period, applicable rate terms and records available. We can first discuss the freight modes and scope without exchanging invoices or private contracts.”

**“How far back can you recover charges?”**
“The usable review window depends on contracts, carrier rules, deadlines and the underlying records. We don't quote a universal lookback or promise an outcome before reviewing the governing terms.”

**“Can you guarantee a refund?”**
“No. A flagged charge, validated finding, approved claim and received credit are different things. We document the basis for a potential issue and pursue recovery only if separately authorized; actual results depend on the evidence and carrier response.”

**“Can I send my invoices to you here?”**
“Please don't put invoices, account numbers or private rate agreements in DMs or comments. Start with non-sensitive details using the contact path at retallyrecovery.com; we'll confirm scope and an approved secure transfer route before requesting records.”

**“Is this all AI?”**
“Automation can help compare and organize records, but it doesn't replace the rate agreement or shipment evidence. Material findings require human review, and recovery actions require separate customer authorization.”

**“This sounds too good to be true.”**
“It's reasonable to ask how the numbers are supported. We separate potential discrepancies from validated findings and actual credited or refunded amounts. You can review the methodology and limitations publicly before deciding whether the initial $0-upfront review is relevant.”

**“How much money will you save us?”**
“That depends on freight spend, contracts, historical records and what has already been corrected or claimed. We won't guess a recovery number or count another auditor's result as ours. The starting point is an agreed review scope.”

**Negative feedback or alleged billing error**
“Thanks for raising this. We won't discuss anyone's private freight or account details in public. Please use the published business contact path with non-sensitive context so it can be reviewed; we can't confirm a factual outcome from a social comment alone.”
Do not imply a case was resolved or independently investigated until it truly was.

## First inquiry triage
- **Stage A — public curiosity:** Give one helpful answer. Do not force a sales pitch.
- **Stage B — interested buyer:** Invite the visitor to the free-audit request page. Public form currently **prepares an email draft**; the visitor must review and press Send. Do not tell the buyer the request was submitted merely because the form opened a draft.
- **Stage C — inquiry received and verified:** Only after the business mailbox shows actual receipt, confirm the sender's general scope (freight modes, approximate volume, carriers, period, existing audit process, available record types). Do not seek invoices or confidential rates through email.
- **Stage D — audit fit:** Operator agrees bounded scope, permitted records, secure intake route and operating identity. No outside carrier contact without explicit authority.
- **Stage E — recovery authorization:** Separate written terms and customer approval before dispute, claim, settlement or other outside action.

## Engagement prompts that fit the brand
- “Where does a freight review slow down: rates, shipment evidence or credits?”
- “Which is harder to reconcile: corrected invoices or accessorial charges?”
- “A duplicated charge and an unreceived credit can look similar. Which does your team see more often?”
- “What makes a freight audit finding credible enough for finance to approve?”

Use as genuine discussion prompts, not repetitive bait. For Instagram, use **link in bio** rather than expecting caption URLs to be clickable. For Facebook, preserve approved tagged landing links. Avoid fabricated polls, screenshots of fake DMs, giveaway tactics or mass commenting.
