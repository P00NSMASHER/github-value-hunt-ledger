03

Questions to ask every freight audit company

  1. What exactly is being audited? Invoices alone, or invoices plus shipment records, rate authority, accessorial evidence, and settlement data?
  2. Is the review pre-pay, post-pay, or both? The control objective changes depending on whether payment has already occurred.
  3. How is controlling rate authority resolved? Ask what happens when the contract, tariff, rate confirmation, or effective date is unclear.
  4. How are corrected invoices and rebills handled? Duplicate-looking records can represent one economic obligation rather than two overcharges.
  5. What requires human review? A provider should be able to explain which ambiguous cases do not auto-convert into dollar findings.
  6. How are prior findings and automatic credits excluded? This is especially important when a TMS, payment provider, or incumbent auditor already exists.
  7. What does the word recovered mean? Ask whether reported recovery is estimated savings, approved claims, posted credits, refunds, or actual realized funds.
  8. How are reversals handled? A later debit, clawback, or reversed credit should change realized recovery.
  9. Who may contact carriers? Audit findings should not silently authorize external claims or settlement activity.
  10. How is the provider paid? Understand setup fees, subscriptions, per-invoice charges, contingency percentages, minimums, and what event makes a fee due.
04

Evidence quality matters more than the number of flags

A useful freight audit finding should be reproducible. At minimum, a reviewer should be able to identify the billed record, the underlying shipment, the commercial source that controlled the charge, the relevant effective date, the expected-charge calculation, and the evidence supporting the disposition.

For recovery work, the evidence chain continues through customer authorization, claim state, carrier response, settlement, and reversals.

Candidate difference ≠ validated finding ≠ approved claim ≠ recovered funds.

Ask providers to show how their reporting keeps those states separate.

05

How freight audit companies price their work

Common structures include subscription or software fees, implementation fees, per-invoice pricing, fixed-fee audits, freight-payment transaction fees, and success-based recovery fees.

For contingency recovery, the important question is the denominator. A percentage of estimated savings is not the same thing as a percentage of eligible actual recovered funds. The engagement should also explain exclusions, prior findings, automatic credits, partial settlements, reversals, taxes, and payment timing.

06

Data and security questions belong in the buying decision

A freight audit can involve invoices, shipment records, rate agreements, payment evidence, and carrier correspondence. Ask where records are transferred, which users can access them, what providers process them, how retention/deletion works, and which security claims are actually evidenced.

A polished sales page is not a substitute for an operating boundary. Freight Recovery publishes a Trust Center and keeps the public marketing site separate from confidential customer-data intake.

If you already audit freight, compare providers differently

A company with mature freight controls should not buy another audit on the assumption that the existing process contributed nothing. Instead, define a frozen historical population and preserve known findings, open claims, automatic credits, and incumbent output before the challenger measures anything.

08

How to choose the right freight audit company

Choose the narrowest model that solves the actual control problem while preserving the evidence and commercial boundaries your finance team needs.

  • Need to stop errors before payment? Prioritize pre-pay audit/payment control.
  • Need to recover historical overpayments? Prioritize post-payment recovery capability.
  • Already have strong controls? Prioritize independent Second-Look attribution.
  • Need enterprise-wide transportation-spend operations? Prioritize platform/integration depth.
  • Need a bounded test before committing? Prioritize a low-friction audit with explicit evidence rules and no forced long-term implementation.
