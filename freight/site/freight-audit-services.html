01

What a freight audit checks

A useful freight audit does more than compare invoice totals. It tests whether a billed charge is supported by the controlling rate, shipment facts and applicable accessorial rules. Common review categories include duplicate invoices or charges, rate-card mismatches, unsupported accessorials, minimum-charge errors, detention or dwell discrepancies, billing-period inconsistencies and settlement or credit mismatches.

Freight Recovery keeps potential recovery, validated findings, approved claims and actual recovered funds separate. A billing difference is not automatically money owed.

02

Evidence needed for a defensible review

The strongest audits combine invoice data with the commercial and operational records that explain what should have happened. Depending on scope, this can include carrier invoices, contracts or rate sheets, shipment records, delivery evidence, accessorial support, prior audit output and payment or credit evidence.

04

Who tends to be a strong fit

Freight-heavy distributors, manufacturers, retailers, multi-site operators, shippers and 3PL customers with multiple carriers or modes are strong candidates. The best populations usually have meaningful invoice volume, accessible rate authority and several months of history.

Small populations can still be worth reviewing when a specific billing issue is suspected, but the audit is intentionally bounded so analysis effort remains proportionate to likely value.
