01

The source question is not “Are there two invoices?”

Carriers may issue a corrected invoice and reversing credit rather than editing an original bill. Two positive invoice numbers for one shipment do not automatically indicate two valid liabilities. A credit merely promised or issued is not proof it reached the customer's ledger.

Review shipment identity, governing rate authority, invoice lineage, actual payments, posted credits and previous auditor findings before assigning a discrepancy.

04

Red flags worth investigating, not automatically disputing

  • Both original and rebill remain economically open without a confirmed offset.
  • A credit memo references a different customer, invoice or shipment.
  • Only a carrier email—not the buyer's ledger—reports the correction.
  • A partial credit is subtracted in full or applied to multiple invoices.
  • An existing auditor or buyer AP team has already accounted for the same correction.
  • A legitimate second leg or service is mistakenly merged into a duplicate.

A suspected discrepancy needs review. A supported finding is separate from a customer-authorized carrier dispute, and both are separate from a realized refund or credit.

05

Credit and rebill FAQ

Do two invoices for one shipment prove double billing?

No. Check whether a reversing credit and corrected bill validly replace the original, and whether there are separate supported services.

Is an issued credit already money recovered?

An issued credit alone does not prove settlement. Verify actual application, refund or eligible realized value under the signed agreement, including reversals.

What if our existing audit provider already found the same issue?

Previously identified findings, open disputes and credits must be excluded from any claimed net-new recovery opportunity.
