01

A potential overcharge is only the beginning

A candidate overcharge can come from rate differences, duplicate billing, unsupported accessorials, shipment-evidence mismatches or missed credits. Before recovery is asserted, the finding should be tied to the controlling commercial source and actual shipment facts.

02

The evidence chain should survive challenge

A strong recovery case preserves the invoice, rate or contractual authority, shipment evidence, calculation, review decision, claim correspondence and later credit, refund or remittance. When evidence is incomplete or contradictory, the amount should remain unresolved instead of being promoted into a recovery total.

03

Recovery attribution prevents double-counting

Pre-existing credits, automatic adjustments, incumbent-known findings and duplicate claims should not generate a new success fee. Actual recovery should be linked once to the validated finding and reduced if a later reversal occurs.

04

Why success-based pricing fits recovery work

The initial Freight Recovery audit is $0 upfront. If a supportable opportunity exists and you authorize recovery work, the agreed fee applies only to eligible funds actually recovered. An estimate or unpaid claim is not feeable merely because it exists.
