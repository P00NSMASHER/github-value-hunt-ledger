The key technical asset is not the UI; it is a paired pay/charge rules engine in which one field timesheet is interpreted under two separate authorities: worker pay rules and client billable-rate rules.
- Concrete capability verified: `computeTimesheetHours` distinguishes pay hours from charge hours for late-start admin, rain-off partial/cancelled, no-work-available, training, leave and public-holiday cases; separate payroll logic handles ordinary/1.5x/2x buckets, RDO accrual, minimum-day top-ups, allowances and casual/worker differences. Client charging separately selects A/B/C Schedule-of-Rates bands and supports line-item UOMs including hour, shift, day, ton, unit, km, m3, m2, lm and each. Rate lookup progressively normalizes role names rather than relying only on exact text matching, because role-name mismatch can otherwise turn directly into margin leakage.
- Source-level authority/evidence chain: Migrations create per-client/per-role `client_rate_cards`, then extend them into full SOR line items with categories such as labour, plant, materials, attachments, allowances and other, including single-rate materials/tipping items. Client-approval utilities route a timesheet to the project site contact first and fall back to the client contact; manual approval records approval timestamp/attribution and moves the timesheet to approved state. Xero payroll push only selects approved employee timesheets, creates a draft pay run, updates matching payslips and marks a timesheet `xero_exported=true` only after the external update succeeds; unmatched employees/calendar memberships are returned as explicit skipped/error results rather than silently treated as successful.
- Important verified limitation: The initial SOR schema has `created_at` but no effective-from/effective-to authority window, and this run found no later source evidence establishing bitemporal/effective-dated rate snapshots. That means a current rate-card mutation could be dangerous for historical rerating unless a separate snapshot mechanism exists elsewhere. The client-invoice/AR side was also not established, so the current strongest proven chain is `field event -> client approval -> expected client charge` plus `field event -> worker pay -> Xero payroll push`, not full customer invoice settlement.
- Evidence quality: Current revision has a successful Vercel deployment status. Real migrations and production functions were inspected. Package scripts include the React/Jest test harness, but targeted source search did not establish a meaningful behavioural test corpus for the money rules in this run.
- Buyer / painful problem: Australian labour-hire, civil, rail, road, water, plant-hire and subcontracting operators paid under client-specific SORs while workers are paid under different award/penalty/RDO/minimum-day rules. Margin disappears when roles map to the wrong rate, night/weekend/public-holiday penalties are paid but not billed correctly, minimum-day or rain-off obligations are not recovered, or approved time fails to reach payroll/billing consistently.

Recompute expected worker pay and expected client charge independently; return a dollar-ranked exception ledger for unmatched/fallback SOR roles, pay-vs-charge band divergence, paid-but-nonbillable scenarios, missing client approval, missing/failed Xero exports and anomalous gross margin.
