### Concrete capability
CocoScout distinguishes three economically different payout outcomes instead of collapsing them into one boolean:
1. transfer never happened (`failed`),
2. transfer genuinely happened (`paid`),
3. money later came back after apparent success (`returned`).

`PayoutBatchItem#mark_returned!` deliberately retains the original negative `payout` ledger entry and posts a positive `reversal`, preserving both historical facts. For `staff_pay` batches the ledger category is `staffing`.

Its Stripe webhook boundary recognizes both `transfer.reversed` and `payout.failed`. A transfer reversal is matched by exact Stripe transfer ID. A connected-account `payout.failed` arrives later, after the platform transfer succeeded, and Stripe only supplies account + amount; the handler therefore changes state only if exactly one paid item matches that payee and amount. Zero or multiple matches trigger a problem notification and no ledger guess.

`PayoutBatchService.return_item!` then:
- posts the compensating return,
- unsets settlement state on contribution sources that expose a reversal hook,
- recomputes the batch so a previously `completed` run becomes `partially_paid` and clears `completed_at`,
- distinguishes cash location: a platform transfer reversal credits org cash, while a payee-bank rejection does not because the money remains in the payee's Stripe Connect balance.

### Verification highlights
The webhook spec explicitly tests `payout.failed — the payee's bank rejected the deposit` and requires:
- item `paid -> returned`,
- original `payout` + compensating `reversal` both retained,
- net ledger effect restored to zero,
- batch `completed -> partially_paid`,
- `completed_at` cleared,
- duplicate webhook redelivery produces only one reversal,
- ambiguous same-amount matches are not guessed.

The same suite tests `transfer.reversed`, including restoration of org cash, and separately proves that a connected-account bank failure must NOT credit org cash.

Staff-pay source code is not a generic payout wrapper. It builds one payee item with auditable contribution rows for worked hours, bonuses, reimbursement and tips; worked time is priced by the employee's effective role, supports hourly versus flat-shift economics, posts worker `earning` ledger entries before payout, and ties included time entries to the payout batch. Tests verify role-specific rates, approved-hours pull-in, component detail, and payout-batch linkage.

Exact-head Actions status: `test`, `lint`, and `scan_js` succeeded. `scan_ruby` failed, so the exact head is not described as all-green.

### Red-team / strongest objection
CocoScout does **not** fully close the target hypothesis. It freezes the resulting contribution amount and preserves source/time-entry linkage, but it does not preserve an explicit event-time pay-rate version or rate snapshot on the approved `staff_time_entry` or contribution. Pricing uses mutable `OrganizationStaffMember` / `StaffRoleQualification` rates when the staff pay run is constructed. A rate change between work approval and run construction can therefore alter the historical economic interpretation without a reconstructable authority version.

Also, a bank return restores the worker ledger balance and reopens the payout batch, but it does not put the original staff time entry back into the generic `unpaid` time pool. Repayment is driven by the restored worker balance / reopened money state, while the original timesheet remains historically tied to the run. That preserves history but is a different semantic than literally resetting the source timesheet.

### Buyer / painful problem / first paid wedge
Buyer: staffing-heavy event production, venue, hospitality, labour-hire, field-service or contractor operations using connected-account payouts.

Pain: an outbound worker payment appears complete, then days later the receiving bank rejects it or the transfer is reversed. Naive systems keep payroll closed, erase the original payment, double-retry, or lose the payable entirely.

First paid wedge: **Payroll Return & Worker-Payable Assurance** — ingest approved work, payout-run state, provider events and accounting/payable state; detect paid→returned contradictions, ambiguous provider matching, reopened worker liabilities, duplicate retry risk and incorrect cash-location treatment.

Money path: prevent unpaid-worker liabilities from disappearing, prevent duplicate re-payouts, reduce reconciliation labor, and make closed payroll periods explainable under later counter-events.

### Why retained
Supersolt supplies the exact temporal-authority primitive CocoScout lacks. When approved timesheet work is staged to payroll, the payroll staging row carries `base_rate_cents`, overtime rate and gross. Payroll aggregation consumes that staged rate and stores `pay_rate_snapshot_cents`, plus award-classification/tax/STP snapshots on the pay-run line.

The payroll service then pushes a deterministic payload to Xero, records push attempts/digests, keeps failures in `xero_push_pending`, and recognizes positive provider states such as finalised, paid, payslips issued, STP lodged, super scheduled and super paid.

### Red-team
No source evidence was found for a later Xero payroll reversal, returned employee bank payment, or external counter-event reopening a previously paid/reconciled pay run. So Supersolt provides **approved-work + event-time pay authority**, but not **revocable payout finality**.

The actual unit tests inspected verify calculation/policy/preflight basics. A design/TDD document mentions a mid-period rate-change test, but that case was not present in the inspected implemented test file; it is not credited as tested.

### Combination value
CocoScout and Supersolt are nearly complementary halves of the missing capability:
- Supersolt: approved work → frozen pay-rate authority → payroll snapshot.
- CocoScout: paid worker obligation → later bank/provider counter-event → compensating ledger → reopened run.

A production-grade labour close system should combine both semantics.

## CLAIMS TESTED

1. **Hypothesis: a real vertical system automatically reopens a worker payout after a bank/provider counter-event.**
   - Supported by CocoScout for `payout.failed` and `transfer.reversed`.

2. **Hypothesis: the same system also pins the exact event-time pay-rate authority to the approved work event.**
   - Falsified for CocoScout. It freezes amount/source linkage but calculates using mutable member/role rates when the pay run is constructed.

3. **Hypothesis: event-time payroll rate authority exists independently in low-attention vertical code.**
   - Supported by Supersolt's `base_rate_cents` payroll staging and `pay_rate_snapshot_cents` pay-run lines.

4. **Hypothesis: provider return should be matched by amount alone.**
   - Falsified. CocoScout acts only when account+amount produces exactly one candidate; ambiguity is surfaced, not guessed.

5. **Hypothesis: all returned money belongs back in the platform/org cash ledger.**
   - Falsified. A connected-account bank rejection can leave money in the payee's provider balance while the worker obligation is reopened; a transfer reversal can return funds to platform cash. Location-of-money is part of authority.

## EMERGING TECHNOLOGY SIGNAL

**Revocable payroll finality** now has independent implementation evidence analogous to the AR-side revocable finality found in Rekoda/Meteroid. The reusable architecture is not merely a `failed` status. It needs:
- original success preserved,
- counter-event identity/provenance,
- compensating ledger entry,
- canonical obligation/run reopened,
- ambiguity that fails closed,
- correct cash-location semantics,
- idempotent redelivery,
- historical event-time economic authority.

The last item remains the missing conjunction in one implementation.

## FAILURE / NEGATIVE KNOWLEDGE

- `payout.failed` handlers that merely alert or change a provider-status field are insufficient.
- A frozen payout amount is not the same as a frozen rate authority/version.
- A pay-rate table, even role-specific, is insufficient if the approved event does not pin the applicable version/rate.
- Resetting source work to unpaid is not always the correct reversal model; preserving historical paid execution while restoring the payable balance can be cleaner, but the system must make repayment discoverable and deterministic.
- Exact money location matters after a reversal: provider balance, platform balance, bank and worker payable are different states.
- CocoScout exact head is not all-green CI because `scan_ruby` failed; do not claim full CI cleanliness.
