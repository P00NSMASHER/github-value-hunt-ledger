### What was newly verified
The hidden high-value artifact is `infra/scripts/provider-payout-reversal-lifecycle-smoke.mjs`, backed by the live earnings/bank-reconciliation implementation.

The smoke executes a provider payout lifecycle from an eligible earning through a paid batch and then through a returned-bank-transfer correction:
- creates an eligible `ProviderEarning` tied to a completed booking;
- batches the earning, moves the batch DRAFT -> PROCESSING -> PAID, and requires maker/approver separation before PAID;
- verifies exactly one payout wallet debit and one balanced accounting journal that debits `partner_wallet_liability` and credits `company_bank_cash`;
- records the actual payout-side bank outflow as a `CompanyBankTransaction`, exposes it as an open monthly-close reconciliation item, then matches it to both the exact payout batch and the exact paid GL entry;
- processes a payout reversal requiring a separate approver, reason, stable reversal reference, and evidence attachment;
- verifies replay/idempotency: the same reversal request returns the same reversal evidence and creates exactly one reversal wallet row / journal;
- verifies the reversal journal is the exact inverse of the original payout journal and restores the provider wallet balance;
- records the **returned payout bank inflow** separately, keeps monthly close open until that bank inflow is reconciled, then matches it to the exact reversal journal and payout batch.

This is materially stronger than a `status='reversed'` flag: the source models both original bank outflow and later returned bank inflow, ties each to accounting evidence, and makes close stay open while either piece is unmatched.

`apps/api/src/settlements/historical-settlement-reconstruction.ts` independently strengthens the authority side. It reconstructs a historical paid settlement only if the retained paid earning, exact platform-fee log, exact tax log, wallet lifecycle, captured payment, completed booking, and booking amounts agree. It carries `platformFeePolicyVersionId`, tax policy version, fee rule snapshot, tax rule snapshot, and source ledger IDs. Missing/ambiguous/mismatched evidence blocks reconstruction instead of falling back to current live policy.

The exact revision also has three successful GitHub Actions checks: `Node, Prisma, API, Admin`, `Customer and Partner Flutter`, and `Migration and paired lifecycle smoke`. The dedicated provider-payout-reversal smoke is present as source/test evidence but is not visibly invoked by the inspected CI workflow, so evidence quality remains 4/5 rather than 5/5.

### Red-team result / strongest objection
This **does not yet fully close CAP-018 / EXP-003**.

The returned-payout correction is an explicit finance/admin reversal operation after bank evidence is available; I did not verify an automatic provider/bank poller that discovers a late contradiction after local PAID when the relevant webhook is permanently lost. Also, the reversal deliberately preserves the original `ProviderEarning` and payout batch as PAID and reopens the provider wallet via a compensating `ADMIN_ADJUSTMENT`; it does not flip the historical earning back to AVAILABLE. That can be a cleaner accounting design, but the safe re-payout path from the restored wallet must be separately proved, including that no current rate/policy can alter the historical amount.

The repository also has separate tests for a different economic counter-event: a customer refund after an earning was already paid creates partner-receivable evidence rather than treating it like a bank-return repayment. That is useful reason-aware semantics: `BANK_RETURN_STILL_OWED` and `REFUND_CLAWBACK_NOT_OWED` must remain distinct.

### Commercial implication
**Payout Finality & Historical Entitlement Audit** becomes more concrete. A read-only assurance product can require four independent proof planes for every supposedly closed worker/provider payout:
1. historical earning/rate/tax authority;
2. payout/ledger posting;
3. bank outflow evidence;
4. any later returned-bank inflow plus exact compensating journal.

This can detect stale PAID states, un-reconciled payout returns, duplicate reversal/re-pay risk, wrong-policy historical reconstruction, and month-close states that appear closed internally while bank evidence remains unresolved.

## COMBINATIONS DISCOVERED
The most useful architecture is now clearer and can be assembled from independently verified patterns:

`Familiarise historical earning authority + Hands historical policy/tax reconstruction + Hands payout-outflow/return-inflow bank reconciliation + an independent terminal provider scanner (Bounty / stripe-connect-reckon pattern) + reason-aware semantics (Mercaria) => authority-preserving revocable payout close.`

The critical new distinction is **observation vs remediation**. The component that notices provider/bank contradiction can remain read-only. The vertical ERP can own the historical earning and compensating accounting. But remediation must be reason-coded:
- `BANK_RETURN_STILL_OWED` => restore/reopen the payable/wallet and allow safe re-payment from original authority;
- `REFUND_CLAWBACK_NOT_OWED` => reduce/recover entitlement instead of re-paying;
- `AMBIGUOUS / UNAVAILABLE` => no economic mutation.

## CLAIMS TESTED
1. **Claim: historical payout economics can be reconstructed without current-policy drift.** Supported by Hands' fail-closed historical settlement reconstruction from retained earning, fee/tax policy versions/rule snapshots, and wallet evidence.
2. **Claim: a payout return can be represented without erasing original success.** Supported by Hands: original PAID source rows are preserved while an exact inverse journal and wallet adjustment record the later returned-money event.
3. **Claim: bank outflow and returned inflow can both participate in month-close proof.** Supported by the payout reversal lifecycle smoke.
4. **Claim: sophisticated scheduled reconciliation automatically re-checks terminal success.** Falsified again by chase-sets and Towing; both normal selectors exclude already-completed/paid payouts.
5. **Claim: every provider payout failure means the worker/provider is still owed.** Falsified by Mercaria's connected-account semantics; physical cash location and underlying entitlement must be separated.
6. **Claim: the exact full conjunction exists in one verified system today.** Not established. Hands still requires an externally triggered/manual correction boundary; independent terminal observation and historical authority remain split across best-of-breed implementations.

## FAILURES / NEGATIVE KNOWLEDGE
- **Reconciliation-selector blindness:** a reconciler that only selects `requested/processing/in-transit` cannot discover a contradiction after local success.
- **Generic reversal ambiguity:** `payout.failed` / `transfer.reversed` is not enough to decide who is economically owed money; distinguish bank return from underlying refund/dispute/clawback.
- **Compensating wallet reopen != proven safe repayment:** restoring balance through an adjustment can be correct, but the re-payment workflow must prove it consumes that restored historical obligation exactly once and does not re-price it.
- **Aggregate provider-balance drift is not transaction identity:** useful alerting evidence, not sufficient authority to mutate a specific payable unless a unique effect can be resolved.
- **Green CI can still omit the exact rare seam:** Hands' exact SHA has successful CI, but the dedicated payout-reversal lifecycle smoke is not visibly wired into the inspected CI job list. Treat the source smoke as strong but not continuously enforced evidence.
