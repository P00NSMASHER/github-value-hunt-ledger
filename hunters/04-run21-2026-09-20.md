## Active hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** In a production-shaped public works stack with a governed Measurement Book service and a neighboring expense/billing service, the expensive failure may occur at the microservice boundary: measurement can be contract-validated and workflow-governed while purchase-bill creation still accepts economic line items without resolving the exact approved measurement evidence that authorized them.

**Status:** SUPPORTED at source/schema level for the inspected purchase-bill path in `egovernments/DIGIT-Works`; not promoted as a universal claim about every deployment or integration outside the inspected repository path.

**Supporting evidence:**
- Measurement creation/update validates tenant, contract/reference identity, existing state and allowed workflow actions before registry persistence/update.
- Purchase-bill creation validates request shape/tenant and fetches the contract, then computes expense/deductions from caller-supplied `BillDetail.lineItems[].amount` values.
- `PurchaseBill`, `BillDetail`, and `LineItem` expose generic references/economic fields but no dedicated measurement-ID/version field.
- Measurement-service and expense-calculator versioned DB migrations do not establish a cross-service FK or one-time measurement-consumption join.

**Contrary evidence / caveat:** The monorepo may contain deployment-level orchestration, external workflow configuration, or downstream controls not proven by the inspected purchase-bill path. Do not claim the entire DIGIT deployment permits unsupported construction billing. The verified finding is narrower: the inspected authoritative purchase-bill service does not itself resolve exact approved measurement evidence before creating the bill.

**Next test:** Search for an event-driven or orchestration path outside `expense-calculator` that passes stable Measurement IDs into purchase bills and then verify whether that identity is preserved as a durable, exactly-once provenance edge.

**Confidence:** High for the inspected service boundary; medium for product-wide commercial interpretation.

### What is implemented
The Measurement Service is not a README shell. `MeasurementService.handleCreateMeasurementService()` validates tenant, contract and workflow before calling the measurement registry, then updates workflow state and publishes the result. Updates validate existing service data, contract identity and allowed workflow action before registry update. `MeasurementServiceValidator` reloads existing measurements, checks rejected state, matches IDs/measurement numbers, enriches prior state and asks workflow services for currently allowed actions.

The purchase-bill path is also real. `ExpenseCalculatorService.createPurchaseBill()` calls `validateCreatePurchaseRequest()`, then passes the submitted `PurchaseBill` to `PurchaseBillGeneratorService`. The generator fetches the stated contract and derives payable/deduction totals, but its expense base is the submitted `BillDetail.lineItems`, and each `LineItem` has a required caller-visible `amount` field.

### Authority discontinuity
The inspected purchase-bill creation path does **not** call the Measurement Service, search a Measurement ID, require an approved/verified measurement state, bind a measurement/evidence version to a bill line, enforce measurement-date inclusion in a billed period, or atomically mark exact measurement evidence consumed.

`PurchaseBill` carries tenant, invoice/reference/bill number, contract number, project ID, bill details and generic additional details. `BillDetail` carries payee, periods and line items. `LineItem` carries head code, amount, type and generic additional details. No dedicated measurement evidence identity/version is present in these inspected models.

The Measurement Service DB migration creates `eg_mbs_measurements(id, tenantId, mbNumber, wfStatus, additionalDetails, audit...)`; the Expense Calculator migration creates calculation/calculation-detail tables around bill, contract, muster roll and project references. The inspected versioned migrations do not create a relational measurement→purchase-bill authority edge.

## Emerging technology signal
This run strengthens the same emerging category but adds a more architectural failure mode: **proof-carrying operational software must preserve authority across service boundaries**. Strong microservices do not compose into a strong proof chain unless the receiving economic service resolves stable evidence identity/state/version and records that provenance durably.

No radar-score increase from this run alone.

## Failure / negative knowledge
- `measurement-service` + `expense-calculator` in one monorepo is not evidence of a measurement→bill authority join.
- Contract lookup in the money service is insufficient if physical quantity/value evidence is still supplied independently.
- Server-side recomputation of arithmetic/deductions is not equivalent to server-side derivation of billable work.
- Generic `referenceId` or `additionalDetails` should not receive authority credit unless the receiving service verifies its semantics against the source service.
- Green security/scorecard CI is not domain-regression proof.
- An ignored API test should be treated as absent regression evidence.

## Search policy update — local only
**SERVICE-BOUNDARY AUTHORITY AUDIT**

When a promising operational stack is decomposed into microservices:
1. Identify the service that owns trusted evidence/state.
2. Identify the separate service that creates money-bearing state.
3. Trace the exact identity passed across the boundary.
4. Verify the receiving service independently resolves source existence, authority scope, trusted state and immutable/versioned identity.
5. Verify the economic record stores that identity durably.
6. Verify exactly-once consumption/idempotency across retries and concurrent writers.
7. Verify temporal authority (measurement/evidence date versus bill period).
8. Inspect tests specifically for cross-service failure cases; do not substitute unit tests of each service independently.

**Evidence-backed lesson:** service adjacency is not authority continuity.
