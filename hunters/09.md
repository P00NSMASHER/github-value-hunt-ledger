### nyimbi/flowforge — government JTBD workflow bundle
- URL: https://github.com/nyimbi/flowforge
- Exact commit / revision: `9b1386998fdea198ae7d70398733bbd947932af3`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong
- What it contains: Substantial Apache-2.0 workflow/orchestration monorepo with a packaged `flowforge-jtbd-gov` domain library. The inspected FOIA/public-record JTBD is a structured machine-readable workflow specifying actor, outcome, stages, data capture, edge cases, required documents, approvals, SLA warnings/breaches, notifications, metrics, NIST compliance tags and data-sensitivity labels.
- Rare or undernoticed value: It encodes public-sector work as auditable workflow data rather than prose. The FOIA spec explicitly models fee categories, exemption codes, Privacy Act identity verification, classified-information escalation, deadline escalation, redaction/search documentation, tiered approvals and backlog/appeal metrics. Package smoke tests load the shipped bundle and assert every JTBD has domain data capture, showing this is integrated structured content rather than a loose note.
- Monetization path: Public-record/FOIA case orchestration for agencies, or a white-label government compliance workflow platform where jurisdiction-specific rule packs drive intake, routing, SLA alerts, approvals, evidence and audit reporting. Could also accelerate internal legal/compliance case management products.
- Likely buyer / user: Municipal/state/federal records offices, government IT integrators, public-sector SaaS vendors, compliance teams and records-management consultants.
- Build-time or data advantage: High for workflow substrate and domain schema; potentially saves months of state-machine, approvals, notification, evidence and domain-bundle design. Statutory rule text/timing must still be validated against current authoritative law before deployment.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Root monorepo/project structure; Apache-2.0 `LICENSE`; `python/flowforge-jtbd-gov/src/flowforge_jtbd_gov/jtbds/foia_request.yaml`; `python/flowforge-jtbd-gov/tests/test_smoke.py`; package search showing README/pyproject integration.
- License / rights: Apache-2.0 for repository code/content at inspected revision. Legal citations and workflow assertions are not a substitute for authoritative/current law.
- Reuse classification: Directly reusable as workflow/schema infrastructure subject to Apache-2.0; legal rule packs require independent validation and jurisdiction/version tracking.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: `flowforge` sounds like a generic workflow framework, while a reusable government-domain workflow library with approvals/SLA/evidence semantics is buried inside the Python workspace.
- Combination opportunities: Combine with request/document parsing, OCR/redaction and a current-law rules registry to create an evidence-first FOIA operations product. The same substrate could power procurement protests, grant administration or other deadline-heavy public-sector workflows.
- Scores (10=high): technical 8; commercial 8; rarity 9; completeness 8; build-time advantage 9; workflow/data advantage 9; high-ticket public-sector fit 8.
- Next action: Inventory all government JTBDs in the package and identify 2-3 monetizable workflows beyond FOIA; for FOIA, externalize federal/state timing/exemption rules into a versioned authoritative rules layer instead of hard-coding them in workflow YAML.

### ihoward40/SintraPrime-Unified — docket/deadline subsystem
- URL: https://github.com/ihoward40/SintraPrime-Unified
- Exact commit / revision: `c0c29a0eb8f404e2bb748be33b8edb9ffff1fc7d`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: rejected
- What it contains: MIT-licensed large application with a docket subsystem implementing case monitoring, PACER/CourtListener client hooks, docket snapshot/change detection, significance/alert settings, extracted deadline objects, a deadline tracker, statutes-of-limitations tables and tests.
- Rare or undernoticed value: There are useful generic engineering patterns for docket snapshots, change hashes, alert callbacks, priority levels and deadline evidence objects; code search also surfaced a dedicated docket test suite. However, the inspected deadline engine hard-codes generic Federal Rules offsets, a simplistic holiday table, and a calculation path that can exclude weekends/holidays throughout the period. That is too risky to use as a legal-deadline authority.
- Monetization path: None from the deadline-calculation logic as-is. Generic non-legal event-monitoring patterns could be reimplemented, but stronger specialized sources should be preferred.
- Likely buyer / user: Legal operations/litigation teams in theory, but the deadline correctness risk blocks recommendation.
- Build-time or data advantage: Medium for generic monitor plumbing; negative for legal deadline rules because incorrect abstractions can create malpractice exposure and rework.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Root substantial application structure; MIT `LICENSE`; `docket/docket_monitor.py` with monitored-case/change/deadline models and PACER hooks; `docket/deadline_tracker.py` with hard-coded FRCP/FRAP offsets, holiday table, calculation and SOL logic; code-search evidence for `docket/tests/test_docket.py` and build summary.
- License / rights: MIT at inspected revision.
- Reuse classification: Do not reuse as a legal deadline engine. At most, selectively reuse generic monitoring infrastructure after independent review; legal time computation must come from an authoritative, versioned rules engine with court/local-rule support.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: The repository contains enough implementation to look production-ready at first glance; deeper inspection reveals that correctness-sensitive deadline arithmetic is more approximate than the surrounding architecture suggests.
- Combination opportunities: None for deadline rules. A safe system could retain the generic event/snapshot model while replacing all deadline math with a separately validated jurisdiction/rules service.
- Next action: Do not spend further hunt time on this deadline engine unless a newer revision replaces the rule model with authoritative versioned rules and proper jurisdiction/local-rule handling.
