## What was implemented and falsified

1. **Policy-specific authority and source truth.** Goods require a goods receipt, services require a service-entry sheet, and explicit direct-invoice authority is separate. `UNAVAILABLE` yields review and zero assertable missing-receipt dollars; only `VERIFIED_EMPTY` can support the planted missing-authority exception.
2. **Reviewed identity with negative blockers.** Aliases can resolve to one canonical entity, later NON_MATCH + replacement MATCH can split/correct a prior identity without deleting history, ambiguous reviewed matches remain blocked, and two same-SKU PO lines cannot establish ownership.
3. **Exact forward conservation.** Partial receipts are consumed on a specific line using integer quantity atoms and integer currency minor units. Quantity and amount are guarded independently; over-allocation is rejected.
4. **Mixed authority cases.** Direct authority, service-entry authority and rejected quantities are separate source events. The precision case uses exact integers (`7,000` quantity atoms and `5,833` minor units), avoiding implicit float/UoM rounding.
5. **Reversible counter-events.** A non-refunding physical return does not change commercial invoice authority. Vendor credit, refunding return and bill cancellation reference the original allocation. A refunding return reduces receipt authority only when its counter-event also reverses billed consumption; re-receipt is a new positive event. Every replay checks nonnegative authority, invoiced balance and residual.
6. **Atomic reverse reservation.** Two concurrent operations each requesting 60% of one allocation produce exactly one winner and one `REVERSE_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` result under `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; they cannot jointly consume 120%.
7. **Crash after target commit.** Broker and target use separate durable SQLite stores. The fixture commits the target mutation, closes the broker before its receipt, reopens it, converts abandoned `DISPATCHING` to `UNKNOWN`, probes the exact downstream key, and records one reversal. A fresh operation ID is blocked after the original becomes APPLIED.
8. **Stable effect identity and payload binding.** Reusing one semantic operation key with changed canonical payload raises `OPERATION_KEY_PAYLOAD_CONFLICT`; same-key replay preserves the derived downstream key.
9. **Honest UNKNOWN and replay expiry.** Mere target absence returns UNKNOWN. UNKNOWN/MANUAL_REVIEW keeps reverse capacity held. The same-key safe-replay predicate becomes false after the stored provider replay deadline; expiry does not become NOT_APPLIED and a fresh operation is rejected.
10. **Positive negative-proof rule.** Capacity is released only after the separate target stores and returns an explicit authoritative negative receipt. An empty target lookup alone cannot release it.
11. **Zombie fencing and synchronous receipt.** State/version compare-and-set rejects a stale worker after recovery. A current synchronous result atomically writes exactly one reversal event and APPLIED state; repeated receipt is fenced.
12. **Bitemporal replay.** A rejection valid on the original receipt date but learned later produces 10,000 atoms in the earlier observation-time replay and 8,000 after the correction becomes known.
