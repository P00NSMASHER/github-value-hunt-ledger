### Why it matters

This repository is valuable because it is close to correct in a way that exposes **two independent post-success failure predicates** that EXP-003 should plant deliberately.

The payout subsystem has several strong primitives: atomic earnings reservation with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, immutable per-earning payout-attempt history, PayPal webhook replay protection, a single shared reconciliation function used by webhook/admin/cron paths, explicit `assumed_complete` vs `completed` vs `denied` state, hourly PayPal reconciliation, denial audit provenance, and retry lineage.

But the source-level control flow reveals two separate finality gaps:

1. **Observation-horizon failure.** `apps/web/app/api/payouts/sync/route.ts` defines `SYNCABLE_STATUSES = ["assumed_complete", "failed", "needs_review"]` and explicitly excludes `completed` and `denied` as terminal. The hourly cron and admin sync therefore stop re-reading PayPal once local status becomes `completed`. A permanently lost later `PAYMENT.PAYOUTS-ITEM.RETURNED` / `REFUNDED` webhook cannot be rediscovered by that scheduled path.
2. **Counter-event transition failure.** `apps/web/lib/payout-reconcile.ts` correctly maps PayPal `RETURNED`, `REFUNDED`, `REVERSED`, `BLOCKED`, `DENIED`, and canceled states to local `denied`, and says those states release earnings. However the release UPDATE only changes `instructor_earnings` when the current earning state is still `payout_pending`. A prior PayPal `SUCCESS` has already changed that earning to `paid`. Therefore a later return/refund/reversal can be observed yet fail to reopen the already-paid earning.

The second defect is economically distinct from the first. Even perfect late-event delivery does not repair the historical obligation if the state-transition predicate only admits the pre-success state.

### External verification

PayPal's first-party Payouts documentation, current as of 2026-09-20, independently confirms that payout items can have `SUCCESS`, `RETURNED`, `REFUNDED`, and `REVERSED` states. `SUCCESS` means funds were credited to the recipient account; `RETURNED` means funds came back to the sender account; `REFUNDED` means the recipient refunded the payout; and the Payouts webhook catalog includes distinct `PAYMENT.PAYOUTS-ITEM.RETURNED` and `PAYMENT.PAYOUTS-ITEM.REFUNDED` events. PayPal also documents unclaimed payouts returning after 30 days. This validates the economic relevance of late counter-events; it does not prove every `SUCCESS` item necessarily transitions later.

### Strongest objection

This is not evidence that the repository has caused real payout loss, and PayPal's docs do not establish a universal `SUCCESS -> RETURNED` transition for every payout scenario. The verified claim is narrower: the application intentionally removes locally completed batches from independent polling, while its return/refund mapping cannot reopen an earning already moved to `paid`. That is enough to make it a high-value negative oracle for systems that must tolerate late counter-events.

## CLAIMS TESTED

- **“Hourly reconciliation + return/refund mappings are enough.” — FALSIFIED.** If `completed` falls out of the polling population, a permanently lost late webhook remains undiscovered.
- **“If a late return is observed, the economic obligation will be repaired.” — FALSIFIED in the inspected path.** The release UPDATE only admits `payout_pending`, while prior success moves the earning to `paid`.
- **“Webhook replay protection implies economic replay safety.” — FALSIFIED as a complete claim.** Event-ID dedupe prevents duplicate processing of the same webhook but does not repair a state-transition predicate that excludes the success state being revoked.
- **“A terminal local status can define the observation horizon.” — FALSIFIED for buyer-grade finality.** The source population used to challenge local success cannot itself be selected only by that mutable local success state.

## EXPERIMENT IMPACT — EXP-003

Add at least these planted cases:

1. `SUCCESS -> local completed/paid`; suppress the later PayPal RETURNED/REFUNDED webhook; scheduled reconciliation must still independently rediscover the counter-event within a defined finality horizon.
2. `SUCCESS -> local completed/paid`; deliver a valid late RETURNED/REFUNDED webhook; provider re-read sees the counter-event; the exact already-paid earning must become outstanding or receive an explicit compensating/reopen event exactly once.
3. Replay the same late return webhook/event and the same provider state; no duplicate reopen/compensation.
4. Mixed batch: one payout item remains successful while another is returned; only the exact historical earning tied to the returned item changes.
5. Completed payout after finality horizon: policy must explicitly state whether observation stops, what evidence supports that horizon, and how later exceptions are handled.

Recommended exception labels:
- `TERMINAL_SUCCESS_NOT_REOBSERVED`
- `LATE_RETURN_DOES_NOT_REOPEN_PAID_EARNING`

## EMERGING TECHNOLOGY SIGNAL

This sharpens RAD-006 / Authority-Aware Money Assurance into three separable capabilities:

1. **Reversal semantics** — can the provider/local model represent the counter-state?
2. **Reversal observability** — can a late counter-state be discovered after local success, even if its webhook is permanently lost?
3. **Economic correction** — can the counter-state idempotently repair/reopen/compensate the exact historical money obligation from the actual success state?

A system can be strong on any two and still be wrong on the third.

## NEGATIVE KNOWLEDGE

Do not award payout-finality credit merely because a repository has `RETURNED`/`REVERSED` enums, webhooks, an hourly `sync`, immutable attempt history, or retry lineage. Inspect exact predicates first:

- Which local statuses enter the polling population?
- Does `force sync` or manual sync bypass that gate?
- When does the scheduler stop?
- What state is the earning/obligation in after success?
- From which current states may the late counter-event transition it?
- Is the counter-event replay-safe at the economic layer, not just webhook-delivery layer?
