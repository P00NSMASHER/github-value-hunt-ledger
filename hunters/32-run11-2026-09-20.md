### Why unusual / CAP-009 delta

RosterSpec already gives this ledger a strong deterministic hard-lock/verifier/repair kernel, but labor/domain rules are external. PayrollEngine demonstrates a mature reusable architecture for selecting the **right rule version for the economic/event date and the knowledge cutoff**. The high-value combination is not to replace RosterSpec with payroll code; it is to adopt/port the authority model:

`official/customer-owned rule source -> versioned rule snapshot (effective_from + known_at/created + source/version/hash) -> compile selected rules into RosterSpec hard constraints -> solve/repair -> verify -> receipt carrying schedule hash + rule snapshot identity + violations/repair delta`.

That turns “the solver honored our configured rule” into the stronger statement “the verifier can show which rule revision governed this shift and why.”

### Verified failure modes

1. The scheduler hard-codes `min_rest_hours=8`, `weekly_rest_hours=32`, `max_weekly_hours=40` in application settings but stores no statute/source identity, effective interval or rule revision.
2. Weekly-rest checking is explicitly described as simplified: it flags only when weekly worked hours exceed `168 - weekly_rest_hours` (136 hours for the default), rather than proving a 32-consecutive-hour free interval or the statutory alternative premium-pay treatment. Synthetic counterexample: seven 8-hour 09:00–17:00 shifts total 56 hours and have no gap longer than 16 hours; the heuristic returns no weekly-rest violation.
3. Current B.C. Employment Standards Act section 36 requires either at least 32 consecutive hours free each week **or** 1.5× regular wage for time worked during that otherwise-free period, and separately requires at least 8 consecutive hours between shifts (emergency exception). The hard-coded heuristic does not encode that complete state/economic consequence.
4. `_get_break_schedule()` adds paid 15-minute rest breaks for >5h and 2×15 minutes for >7h while current B.C. government guidance states a 30-minute unpaid meal break after more than five consecutive hours and that employers are not required by Employment Standards to provide coffee breaks. Company/collective-agreement rules may add breaks, but that is a different authority.
5. Most importantly, detected rest/role violations are **not hard constraints**: `_allocate_shift_type()` sets violation flags/reasons and still saves the DRAFT shift. The supplied scheduler test prints WARNING and “Manager must review and override before publishing” but returns success (`0`) even when violations exist.

### Negative knowledge

`hard-coded number + compliance label + warning flag` is not schedule authority. CAP-009 must distinguish:
- authoritative rule identity/currentness,
- correct rule semantics,
- rule outcome (BLOCK vs premium-pay consequence vs REVIEW), and
- solver/verifier enforcement.

A schedule can be mathematically feasible while being legally stale, semantically wrong, or merely “warned” rather than prevented.

## Combination / experiment impact

Proposed CAP-009 acceptance contract:

1. `RULE_SOURCE`: official/customer-owned source identity and retrieval/approval time.
2. `RULE_VERSION`: deterministic version/hash + `effective_from/effective_to` + `known_at`.
3. `RULE_SCOPE`: jurisdiction, worker class, agreement/exemption/override scope.
4. `RULE_SEMANTIC`: executable invariant with explicit outcome type (`BLOCK`, `PREMIUM_PAY`, `REVIEW`, `ALLOW`).
5. `SCHEDULE_SUBJECT`: immutable schedule/shift identity + event date/time/timezone.
6. `VERDICT`: PASS/FAIL/REVIEW plus rule IDs and evidence.
7. `REPAIR`: minimum-disruption candidate from RosterSpec, never silently weakening a hard rule.
8. `RECEIPT`: schedule hash + authority bundle hash + verifier/solver version + evaluation timestamp.
