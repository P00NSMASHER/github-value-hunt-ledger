- Concrete capability: GPL-3.0-only evidence sealing library that canonicalizes a manifest, hashes files, builds a Merkle root, supports previous-seal chaining and Ed25519 signatures, and—most importantly for this hunt—implements **RFC 3161 external timestamp requests and offline verification** against a supplied trusted TSA certificate.

Verification checks the TSTInfo message imprint against the expected manifest, SHA-256 algorithm, CMS signer identity/signature, the signed `messageDigest`, timestamping EKU and certificate validity at `genTime`. Tests deliberately fail wrong-manifest, untrusted-certificate, missing-EKU and invalid-time cases.
- Likely buyer / user: Regulated SaaS, audit/compliance providers, MSP/MSSPs, recovery-evidence services, incident-response/eDiscovery teams and cyber-insurance assurance programs that need independently timestamped proof packages.
- Painful problem: A producer can generate a perfectly self-consistent signed evidence bundle after the fact unless there is some independently controlled evidence that the digest existed at a particular time.
- Monetization mechanism / likely first paid wedge: Add a **Recovery Evidence Seal** to a fixed-price Recovery Readiness Audit or recurring Continuous Assurance service. Hash/sign the restore evidence locally, obtain an external RFC 3161 timestamp, and ship the TSA response/certificate plus offline verification result in the auditor/insurer package.
- Build-time or data advantage: Roughly 2–4 months saved on DER/CMS/TSTInfo handling, timestamp request/response parsing, signer resolution, EKU/time validation, Ed25519/Merkle manifest plumbing and negative tests.
- Evidence inspected: exact revision and tree; `evidence_seal/timestamp.py`, `evidence_seal/manifest.py`, `tests/test_timestamp.py` and root license. Source verifies the RFC 3161 imprint and CMS signature rather than trusting a timestamp-service response string. Important limitation: the implementation validates against a supplied/pinned TSA certificate and does not itself build a full PKIX chain or perform OCSP/CRL/network revocation checking, so production trust policy must handle that separately.
- License / rights: GPL-3.0-only verified at the exact revision.
- Reuse classification: Copyleft. Direct use in a distributed/proprietary product needs deliberate GPL compliance or separate permission; the protocol/verification behavior can also guide an independent implementation using RFC 3161 standards and other permissive libraries.
- Why it is non-obvious: The small “evidence seal” framing hides a real timestamp-token verifier with adversarial certificate/message tests; the commercially relevant asset is independent time-existence proof, not another local evidence ledger.
- Score: A 4 / B 4 / C 5 / D 5 / E 5 / F 4 = **27/30**. Rights clarity is strong but copyleft reduces proprietary reuse flexibility; evidence/completeness is high because both request and negative verification behavior were inspected directly.
- Combination opportunities: `backupdrill/cli` / FireDrill performs the real restore and invariants -> OpenWright/PRC binds/signs the exact evidence -> `evidence-seal` RFC 3161 anchors the final digest externally -> Evidentia/OSCAL packages the control result. - Next action: Timestamp one synthetic signed recovery bundle, then test altered manifest, wrong TSA certificate, certificate outside `genTime`, TSA outage and replay.
