### Emiel2/Thesis-Surgery-Scheduling — emergency-robust operating-room scheduling MIP
- URL: https://github.com/Emiel2/Thesis-Surgery-Scheduling
- Exact commit / revision: `6e4bd926fdb378884f18704bccbdbe441ae4773e`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong
- What it contains: A compact but substantive Java/CPLEX research implementation for comparing elective-only operating-room schedules with emergency-robust schedules. The ~109 KB `MIP.java` models multiple ORs, elective surgery durations, regular/idle/overtime cost weights, a Break-In-Moments overlap constraint intended to cap emergency wait to roughly two hours, stochastic emergency arrival counts/categories/times/durations, and rescheduling after one or multiple emergency arrivals.
- Rare or undernoticed value: Most public surgery-scheduling examples optimize elective throughput only. This code explicitly reserves resilience for unpredictable emergency arrivals and then simulates/reschedules against realized emergencies, which is much closer to the planning trade-off hospitals face between utilization and surge responsiveness.
- Monetization path: Clean-room decision-support/simulation product for OR capacity planning that lets perioperative leaders compare utilization, overtime and emergency-response robustness under their own duration/arrival assumptions. Keep it advisory/planning rather than autonomous clinical scheduling until independently validated.
- Likely buyer / user: Hospital perioperative operations teams, surgery centers with emergency coverage, healthcare OR consultancies, hospital-operations software vendors.
- Build-time or data advantage: Compresses several weeks of formulation work by exposing a concrete emergency-resilience objective, event simulation and rescheduling structure, but production use still needs independent reimplementation, modern solver choices, calibration, UI/data integration and rigorous clinical/operational validation.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Root contains `ArrivalTime.java`, `CplexResult.java`, `ScheduleInstance.java` and a ~109 KB `MIP.java`; source comments define the BIM constraint and operating/idle/overtime objective; implementation samples emergency counts, arrival windows and duration categories and includes sequential rescheduling for multiple emergencies.
- License / rights: No repository license detected at the inspected revision. IBM CPLEX is a separately licensed commercial solver dependency.
- Reuse classification: INSPECT / LEARN / CLEAN-ROOM ONLY unless the author grants rights; independently reimplement the formulation and validate source literature/licensing before commercial use.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: Generic thesis name, only four source files and no product framing hide a surprisingly deep single-file MIP/simulation implementation.
- Combination opportunities: Pair the emergency-resilience formulation with a permissive modern optimization stack and a healthcare operations dashboard; conceptually combine with capacity forecasting, historical surgery-duration distributions and m8flow-style human approval workflows rather than reusing this source.
- Next action: Recreate the model independently on synthetic OR data using a permissively deployable solver, then compare elective utilization/overtime against emergency wait/resilience before approaching any healthcare workflow.

### panoskom/PPDM_framework — uncertainty-aware post-prognosis maintenance scheduling with Value of Information
- URL: https://github.com/panoskom/PPDM_framework
- Exact commit / revision: `4fbca1dfc28280d0e6428b22c796e15c4f305ccd`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong
- What it contains: A zero-star MIT research implementation for post-prognosis maintenance decision-making over multiple components. The Gym environment represents remaining useful life, imperfect-repair history, replacement/discard decisions, finite maintenance-slot capacity and uncertainty; actions are per-component hold / imperfect repair / replace. The repository also contains uncertainty decomposition, an "I don't know" deferral mechanism, a learned safety layer, a Value-of-Information loop for deciding when fresh sensor/prognostic data is worth acquiring, evaluation code, plotting code and a dataset-free demo.
- Rare or undernoticed value: Most predictive-maintenance repositories stop at RUL prediction. This one implements the harder downstream decision problem: when to hold, imperfectly repair or replace under capacity constraints, while explicitly modeling uncertainty and the cost/value of collecting new information. That makes it potentially useful as an industrial decision-support reference rather than another prognostics model.
- Monetization path: Build a maintenance-planning decision-support module that ingests a customer's existing RUL/condition estimates and compares maintenance schedules, inspection timing and imperfect-repair/replacement trade-offs. Sell as a pilot to industrial asset operators or condition-monitoring vendors where avoided downtime and avoided unnecessary inspections are measurable.
- Likely buyer / user: Industrial maintenance/reliability teams, aerospace/energy/manufacturing asset operators, predictive-maintenance vendors and industrial analytics integrators.
- Build-time or data advantage: Saves several weeks to months of research-prototype work around the environment, reward/state formulation, capacity-constrained action model, uncertainty deferral and VoI loop. Production deployment still requires domain calibration, deterministic baselines and independent validation on the buyer's equipment/failure process.
- Concrete evidence inspected: `ppdm_env.py` (~41 KB) implements the multi-component Gym environment, RUL uncertainty, slot/capacity state, reward decomposition and capacity/RUL safety checks; `safety_layer.py` implements a constraint model and safety-oriented environment; repository tree includes `evaluate_agent.py`, `create_expert_transitions.py`, `main.py`, plotting artifacts and an example rollout. The terminal commit documents a four-component fatigue case study, a 60-day horizon and a VoI loop that can skip data collection when its estimated value is non-positive.
- License / rights: MIT; root `LICENSE` present at the inspected revision. Research paper/data claims remain subject to their own provenance and validation requirements.
- Reuse classification: Permissive code reuse subject to MIT terms; treat the learned policy, numerical results and safety claims as research evidence only until independently reproduced.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: Zero stars, academic terminology and a prognostics paper framing obscure the commercially interesting layer: maintenance actions, scarce service slots, uncertainty and inspection-value decisions are all represented in executable code.
- Combination opportunities: Pair with Flowcept/telemetry normalization for event ingestion, RulesEngine-style deterministic guardrails, and an evidence/case workflow for human approval. It could also complement condition-monitoring products that already predict RUL but do not optimize the action/inspection schedule.
- Next action: Run the dataset-free demo and independently benchmark the policy against simple deterministic threshold and optimization baselines on a public maintenance dataset; retain only decision gains that reproduce before turning this into a buyer-facing pilot.

### usnistgov/rmellipse — NIST correlated-uncertainty and metrological-traceability engine
- URL: https://github.com/usnistgov/rmellipse
- Exact commit / revision: `79daadef817c892e0366cd748279665c6f1f8dd3`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong / MASTER contender
- Concrete capability: Rocky Mountain Ellipse (RME), developed under the NIST CHIPS Metrology program, provides an executable digital record of metrological traceability with correlated uncertainty propagation. It uses structured measurement objects/arrays, supports finite-difference and Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation through arbitrary Python functions, keeps covariance/correlation information rather than collapsing everything into independent error bars, and serializes measurement records to inspectable formats including HDF5.
- Rare or undernoticed value: Most lab automation projects move samples and results; they do not preserve the mathematical uncertainty chain behind a measurement. RME addresses the harder audit/recalculation problem: which calibration/measurement inputs produced a reported result, what uncertainties were correlated, and how those uncertainties propagate through downstream calculations.
- Likely buyer / user: Semiconductor metrology and process-development labs, RF calibration labs, instrument vendors, accredited/calibration laboratories, regulated engineering organizations.
- Painful problem: Measurement results often live in spreadsheets/scripts with weak provenance; repeating an uncertainty budget, tracing calibration ancestry or defending a measurement during an audit can require expert manual reconstruction.
- Monetization mechanism: Fixed-price metrological-traceability implementation first, then an on-prem/lab SaaS module that captures measurement inputs, covariance/uncertainty models and recalculable provenance beside instrument results.
- First paid wedge: Take one existing lab measurement/calibration chain that is currently maintained in scripts/spreadsheets and produce a machine-recalculable uncertainty/provenance package; measure time saved during recalculation, method transfer or audit review.
- Build-time or data advantage: Saves months of uncertainty-propagation/data-model discovery and provides a government-developed reference for correlated measurement records rather than forcing a commercial team to invent a covariance-aware schema from scratch.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Exact terminal revision; NIST licensing statement; README and documentation describing metrological traceability and uncertainty propagation; recursive tree with extensive example notebooks/scripts, schema examples and a multi-megabyte HDF5 measurement artifact. The repository is pre-1.0 and explicitly remains research software, so production reliability has not been assumed.
- License / rights: NIST Software Licensing Statement permits use, copying, distribution, modification and derivative works with the notice preserved and NIST acknowledged; NIST-developed employee software is stated as not subject to U.S. copyright protection. External dependencies/data remain separately governed.
- Reuse classification: Broadly reusable under the NIST notice, subject to preserving attribution/notice and independently reviewing dependencies. Treat numerical/production fitness as unvalidated until reproduced.
- Why it is non-obvious: It is framed as NIST research metrology software rather than a lab-operations product. The commercial value is not the name “ellipse” but the data model for correlated uncertainty and recalculable traceability.
- Value score: A 3/5, B 4/5, C 5/5, D 5/5, E 4/5, F 5/5 = **26/30**. Revenue requires buyer-specific lab integration, but domain compression, rarity and rights are unusually strong.
- Combination opportunities: Add to the existing lab/instrument cluster as the uncertainty/provenance math layer beneath PyTestLab/PyDCC/Galago/Flowcept-style orchestration. A stronger stack becomes instrument automation + calibration lineage + correlated uncertainty + signed evidence, rather than only instrument control.
- Next action: Reproduce one documented uncertainty example and serialization round-trip, then benchmark the model against a simple GUM-style spreadsheet on a public RF or semiconductor-metrology example before any production claim.
