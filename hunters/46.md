### rootcoder007/morie
- URL: https://github.com/rootcoder007/morie
- Exact commit / revision: `ec931fe20a8889a3760ff59632ecceaad28c56df`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: watch
- What it contains: Large Python + R scientific inference toolkit with in-package causal methods including nearest/Mahalanobis/exact/CEM/optimal/genetic/cardinality matching, double/debiased machine learning, clustered standard errors, R-learner causal forests, T/S/X/DR meta-learners, design-based GLM and causal-DAG tooling. It is packaged for PyPI/R, has CI/CodeQL and Docker distribution rather than being only notebooks.
- Rare or undernoticed value: The unusually valuable piece is operationally credible causal inference under clustered dependence. `src/morie/dml_clustered.py` is a general policy-evaluation path for settings such as flights nested in corridors, students in schools or patients in hospitals, where naive independent-observation standard errors can be anti-conservative. The breadth of matching and meta-learner implementations in one cross-language codebase is also unusual.
- Monetization path: Causal/policy evaluation module for enterprise experimentation, operations-policy analysis, pricing/service changes or regulated program evaluation where observations are naturally clustered and ordinary A/B inference is unreliable. Because of the license, the cleanest commercial routes are an AGPL-compliant open service, a separately isolated service with careful obligations, or independent implementation of public statistical methods rather than proprietary embedding of modified covered code.
- Likely buyer / user: Experimentation/decision-science teams, policy/operations analysts, research consultancies, organizations evaluating interventions across stores/sites/regions/cohorts.
- Build-time or data advantage: Months of implementation/cross-validation reference across causal estimators, clustered variance, matching and DAG workflows; useful as a benchmark/reference even if the licensing architecture prevents direct closed-source embedding.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Pinned README describing implemented native estimators and package/distribution paths; code search and actual source for `src/morie/dml_clustered.py` with AGPL SPDX and general clustered-DML rationale; corresponding R implementation surfaced; current commit inspected.
- License / rights: Python and R software are AGPL-3.0-or-later; optional kernel adjuncts are GPL-2.0-only; README states papers/data/documentation are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 unless separately marked. Data/content rights must be handled independently from code rights.
- Reuse classification: Reusable with strong copyleft conditions; proprietary/network use of modified AGPL-covered software requires careful compliance. Statistical ideas in the cited literature may be independently implemented, but repository code/content should not be copied into a closed product without satisfying licenses.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: Broad “research toolkit” positioning hides commercially useful production-style causal engines and clustered-inference routines; the repository spans many domains and is easy to dismiss as academic miscellany.
- Combination opportunities: Pair clustered causal inference with forecasting/optimization products to measure whether operational policies actually cause KPI changes, rather than merely correlating with them. A compelling decision-system stack could use forecasting to predict, optimization to recommend, and DML to estimate realized treatment effects across sites.
- Next action: Validate the clustered-DML implementation on a lawful synthetic/public benchmark with known treatment effect and within-cluster correlation, comparing interval coverage against DoubleML or another established reference before promoting beyond watch status.

### khalil-research/PyEPO
- URL: https://github.com/khalil-research/PyEPO
- Exact commit / revision: `6a9fa25ce3b1c25ac5295b44707adf66a016929e`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong
- What it contains: MIT PyTorch/JAX library for end-to-end predict-then-optimize learning. Instead of training a forecast only on prediction error, it supports losses that optimize downstream decision regret over LP/MIP problems. Implemented families include SPO+, PG, DPO/PFYL/I-MLE/AI-MLE, RFWO/RFYL, DBB/NID, CaVE, NCE/CMAP and learning-to-rank methods, with Gurobi, COPT, Pyomo, OR-Tools and MPAX backends plus a symbolic LP/MIP DSL.
- Rare or undernoticed value: It is a mature bridge between machine learning and operations research: predictions can be trained for the quality of the actual routing/knapsack/scheduling/resource decision they induce. CaVE uses binding-constraint geometry for binary programs, while caching/parallel solve infrastructure attacks the major training-cost bottleneck.
- Monetization path: Decision-focused forecasting/optimization products for inventory, pricing, energy dispatch, routing, scheduling and capacity allocation where a small prediction-error improvement may be economically irrelevant but regret/cost reduction is measurable.
- Likely buyer / user: OR/ML platform teams, supply-chain and energy optimization vendors, pricing/revenue-management teams, route/scheduling software builders.
- Build-time or data advantage: Saves months of differentiable-optimization loss implementations, solver-backend plumbing, multiprocessing/solution caching and JAX/PyTorch integration. It also provides experiment notebooks spanning shortest path, CVRP/TSP/knapsack and real-world energy scheduling.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Pinned README at the exact revision; source search hit for `pkg/pyepo/func/surrogate.py` implementing `SPOPlus`; JAX mirror implementation; `pkg/tests/test_50_func.py` tests SPO+ subgradients, `pkg/tests/test_55_jax.py` checks a closed-form SPO+ subgradient across backends, CUDA and backend-pipeline tests exist, and the CaVE notebook constructs `optDatasetConstrs`/binding sets and compares SPO+/CaVE variants. The README's reported CVRP speedups are treated as repository-reported benchmark results, not independently reproduced here.
- License / rights: MIT, verified from repository metadata/README. Commercial solver backends such as Gurobi/COPT have their own licenses; open backends such as OR-Tools/Pyomo paths can be selected separately.
- Reuse classification: Core directly reusable under MIT; solver/backend licensing remains independent.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: More visible than the lane's obscure finds, but the commercially important capability—training forecasts against actual downstream economic decisions—is still rare in production planning stacks and much harder to build correctly than generic ML inference.
- Combination opportunities: Pair with `Ahmad-Mujtaba-09/demand-forecasting-agent` or Durbyn so demand models optimize stockout/holding cost instead of WMAPE alone; use MIP++ as a fast decision backend for repeated optimization; add `morie` only as a causal post-deployment evaluator under an appropriate license architecture.
- Next action: Build a small lawful inventory/replenishment benchmark comparing ordinary MSE/WMAPE training against SPO+/another PyEPO loss on the same downstream cost model, reporting inventory dollars/service level/regret rather than forecast accuracy alone.

### iutzeler/skwdro
- URL: https://github.com/iutzeler/skwdro
- Exact commit / revision: `0c7d52557fa4c90bd9b0f3ecff619403c347c400`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong
- What it contains: BSD-3-Clause Python library for Wasserstein distributionally robust machine learning with scikit-learn-style estimators and PyTorch dual solvers. Of particular commercial relevance, it includes a real `NewsVendor` estimator with a dedicated mathematical-programming solver and entropic Torch variants rather than only generic DRO primitives.
- Rare or undernoticed value: It makes robust inventory decisions look like ordinary estimator fitting: order quantity is learned against an ambiguity set around observed demand instead of assuming the empirical distribution is exactly correct. That is a useful hedge against sparse data, distribution shift and launch/new-SKU uncertainty where ordinary quantiles can be brittle.
- Monetization path: Robust replenishment/procurement module sold to distributors, MRO/spares operators or manufacturers as a downside-risk reduction layer; measure avoided stockout/overstock cost under demand shift rather than abstract statistical accuracy.
- Likely buyer / user: Inventory optimization vendors, supply-chain analytics teams, new-product planners, energy/operations teams facing distributional uncertainty.
- Build-time or data advantage: Provides transport-cost abstractions, Wasserstein dualization, dedicated and entropic solvers, sklearn-compatible estimator APIs and existing tests, avoiding a large amount of fragile DRO derivation/solver plumbing.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Repository metadata shows BSD-3-Clause and current 2026 activity. The pinned `skwdro/operations_research/_newsvendor.py` implements input validation, robustness radius, buy/sell economics, dedicated `WDRONewsvendorSpecificSolver`, entropic Torch dualization, fit/eval/score, and explicit solver selection. Tests instantiate both dedicated and entropic NewsVendor variants.
- License / rights: BSD-3-Clause, verified from GitHub license metadata at the inspected revision.
- Reuse classification: Permissively reusable subject to BSD-3-Clause notice conditions; dependencies/solvers remain separately licensed.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: Only about 25 stars and framed as a research/statistical-learning package; the directly monetizable inventory-risk component is buried under the generic DRO umbrella.
- Combination opportunities: Pair with the intermittent-demand forecasting stack as a robust decision layer when historical demand is sparse or shifting; benchmark against PyEPO decision-focused learning, where PyEPO optimizes predictive decisions and skwdro protects against distributional misspecification.
- Next action: Run a lawful inventory benchmark with synthetic/real demand shifts, comparing empirical newsvendor, PyEPO-trained decisions and skwdro's Wasserstein NewsVendor on service level, holding cost, shortage cost and worst-case regret.

### saurabac/Wasserstein-Data-Markets
- URL: https://github.com/saurabac/Wasserstein-Data-Markets
- Exact commit / revision: `9f62daa73ae44129458969b6a6cb8bb4ebce9948`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: watch
- What it contains: MIT research implementation of data-market mechanisms for differentially private data. The actual `models/_markets.py` builds coalition value functions and implements Shapley allocation, Bayesian virtual costs, central procurement, budget-constrained procurement, greedy knapsack-style selection, random baselines and a single-minded privacy-constrained auction solved with CVXPY/MOSEK. Related loss code includes newsvendor/data-rate objectives.
- Rare or undernoticed value: The combination of privacy-aware data valuation, coalition effects and explicit seller payments is unusual. It is closer to a programmable procurement mechanism for training/forecast data than a generic data-marketplace UI.
- Monetization path: Potential R&D substrate for data consortia, federated-learning marketplaces, privacy-preserving benchmarking or enterprise data-procurement tools that need to decide which data contributors are worth buying under a budget.
- Likely buyer / user: Data-sharing consortia, federated-learning vendors, research marketplaces, privacy engineering teams and organizations purchasing third-party datasets/features.
- Build-time or data advantage: Provides concrete mechanism-design implementations and coalition/Shapley accounting that would otherwise require substantial economics/optimization work.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Exact MIT repository/commit; `models/_markets.py` constructs all coalitions, computes Shapley values and implements `Central`, `BudgetedMarket`, `GreedyKnapsack`, `CentralBudget`, `Random` and `SingleMindedQuery`, including CVXPY/MOSEK optimization. No conventional pytest/unittest suite was found in the inspected repository search.
- License / rights: MIT for repository software. Any experiment datasets, papers or externally sourced benchmark data remain separately governed.
- Reuse classification: Code permissively reusable under MIT, but dependency licensing (notably MOSEK for some solver paths) and dataset rights must be handled independently.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: Three stars and notebook-heavy presentation make it look like a narrow paper artifact; the market-mechanism code is much more concrete than the repository visibility suggests.
- Combination opportunities: Combine with PyEPO/skwdro-style decision value to price data by downstream economic improvement rather than generic model accuracy; possible foundation for a “which vendor data should we buy?” optimization product.
- Next action: Keep at watch until its mechanisms are validated on a small synthetic procurement benchmark with an open solver and tested for scaling beyond exhaustive `2^N - 1` coalition enumeration.
