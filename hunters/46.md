## Catalog rule

### khalil-research/PyEPO
- URL: https://github.com/khalil-research/PyEPO
- Exact commit / revision: `6a9fa25ce3b1c25ac5295b44707adf66a016929e`
- Date inspected: 2026-09-19
- Status: strong
- What it contains: MIT PyTorch/JAX library for end-to-end predict-then-optimize learning. Instead of training a forecast only on prediction error, it supports losses that optimize downstream decision regret over LP/MIP problems. Implemented families include SPO+, PG, DPO/PFYL/I-MLE/AI-MLE, RFWO/RFYL, DBB/NID, CaVE, NCE/CMAP and learning-to-rank methods, with Gurobi, COPT, Pyomo, OR-Tools and MPAX backends plus a symbolic LP/MIP DSL.
- Rare or undernoticed value: It is a mature bridge between machine learning and operations research: predictions can be trained for the quality of the actual routing/knapsack/scheduling/resource decision they induce. CaVE uses binding-constraint geometry for binary programs, while caching/parallel solve infrastructure attacks the major training-cost bottleneck.
- Monetization path: Decision-focused forecasting/optimization products for inventory, pricing, energy dispatch, routing, scheduling and capacity allocation where a small prediction-error improvement may be economically irrelevant but regret/cost reduction is measurable.
- Likely buyer / user: OR/ML platform teams, supply-chain and energy optimization vendors, pricing/revenue-management teams, route/scheduling software builders.
- Build-time or data advantage: Saves months of differentiable-optimization loss implementations, solver-backend plumbing, multiprocessing/solution caching and JAX/PyTorch integration. It also provides experiment notebooks spanning shortest path, CVRP/TSP/knapsack and real-world energy scheduling.
- Concrete evidence inspected: Pinned README at the exact revision; source search hit for `pkg/pyepo/func/surrogate.py` implementing `SPOPlus`; JAX mirror implementation; `pkg/tests/test_50_func.py` tests SPO+ subgradients, `pkg/tests/test_55_jax.py` checks a closed-form SPO+ subgradient across backends, CUDA and backend-pipeline tests exist, and the CaVE notebook constructs `optDatasetConstrs`/binding sets and compares SPO+/CaVE variants. The README's reported CVRP speedups are treated as repository-reported benchmark results, not independently reproduced here.
- License / rights: MIT, verified from repository metadata/README. Commercial solver backends such as Gurobi/COPT have their own licenses; open backends such as OR-Tools/Pyomo paths can be selected separately.
- Reuse classification: Core directly reusable under MIT; solver/backend licensing remains independent.
- Why it is not already obvious / crowded: More visible than the lane's obscure finds, but the commercially important capability—training forecasts against actual downstream economic decisions—is still rare in production planning stacks and much harder to build correctly than generic ML inference.
- Combination opportunities: Pair with `Ahmad-Mujtaba-09/demand-forecasting-agent` or Durbyn so demand models optimize stockout/holding cost instead of WMAPE alone; use MIP++ as a fast decision backend for repeated optimization; add `morie` only as a causal post-deployment evaluator under an appropriate license architecture.
- Next action: Build a small lawful inventory/replenishment benchmark comparing ordinary MSE/WMAPE training against SPO+/another PyEPO loss on the same downstream cost model, reporting inventory dollars/service level/regret rather than forecast accuracy alone.
