## Adversarial economics, not observed results
Assume 100 jobs, 5 minutes of manual work per job, $35/h labor, a $100 fixed audit fee, and no acquisition costs. A *full* 100-job manual review would cost **$291.67**, above the fee. A **two-job first-pass ID triage** would cost **$5.83** under the same artificial per-job assumption; that is **not** the cost of completing the audit. The estimated **$285.83 gap** is merely a *possible avoided unnecessary first-pass triage scenario* if independent evidence permits safe post-triage resolution. Because the actual bridge still holds 100/100, **no observed labor reduction or commercial margin gain is claimed**.

**New falsifier:** `invoice_line_id` might be only *unique within an invoice document*, not globally across invoices. Two independently numbered invoices could each contain line `1`; unscoped duplication would trigger a mistaken collision/review. Our toy schema has no authenticated invoice-document identity or source/namespace contract. A diagnostic collision is therefore *suspected aliasing*, **not proof of duplicate billing**. Next version must establish authoritative composite identity and red-team cross-invoice same-number counterexamples before making any review-cost promises. Conversely a composite key must never hide duplicates within the *same* invoice.
